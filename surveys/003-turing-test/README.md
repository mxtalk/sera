## text

text.md holds the article, as a desktop reader receives it, in page order, from the title to the end of "Conferences". Each piece sits on its own line, numbered from p1.

Left out:

- Everything after "Conferences": See also, Notes, References, Further reading, External links.
- As in surveys 001 and 002: hatnotes ("Not to be confused with …", the redirect and other-uses notes, "Main article: …", "See also: …"), the infobox, the artificial intelligence sidebar, the short description, categories, language links, edit links, and site menus and notices.

Kept as readers see them: footnote markers such as [3], the [citation needed] tag, and square brackets that the article puts inside quotations, as in "[H]ow many" and "[the] average interrogator".

Changed:

- Non-breaking spaces are plain spaces, and repeated white space is one space. Line breaks inside a caption are spaces.
- Bold and italics are dropped; captions are in *italics*.
- Markdown marks show each piece's form: `# p1` title, `## p5`, `### p6` and `#### p46` headings, `p20 *…*` image captions, `> p8 …` block quotations (one piece for each paragraph of a quotation), and `- p86 …` indented lines (Turing's sample dialogues, and the two entries under "Human intelligence vs. intelligence in general" with the lines beneath them) and bullets.

## fire list

fires.csv has a row for each *the*, *and* and *of* in text.md, as a whole word: 1,061 fires, *the* 576, *of* 302, *and* 183.

No word in this text is mentioned rather than used, so every whole-word *the*, *and* and *of* is a fire. That includes the "[the]" the article adds inside a quotation in p99.

If the list misses a fire or includes a word that isn't one, flag it as a list error, as survey.md says.

**original** is the sentence holding the fire. These pieces stand whole: the title and headings.

Elsewhere, a sentence ends at . ! ? or …, after any closing quote, bracket or footnote marker, where a space and then a capital letter follow, with or without an opening quote or bracket. The split skips these abbreviations: U.S., e.g., i.e., etc., vs., ca., No., Dr., Mr., Mrs., St., Inc., pp., p., ed. It also skips a capital initial that stands next to another initial or after a capitalised word, as in Stanley G. Weinbaum, E. T. A. Hoffmann and R. D. Hinshelwood. Block quotations and indented lines are split like paragraphs.
